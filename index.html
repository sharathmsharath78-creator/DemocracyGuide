The Complete Process

How the world's largest democracy works, step-by-step.

The Election Commission

The Election Commission of India (ECI) is an autonomous constitutional authority responsible for administering election processes in India at national, state and district level.

Types of Elections

  • Lok Sabha: General elections for the lower house (PM).
  • Rajya Sabha: Upper house, elected by state legislators.
  • Vidhan Sabha: State assembly elections (CM).
  • Local Bodies: Panchayats and Municipalities.

Voter Registration

Any Indian citizen above 18 years can vote. Registration is done via Form 6 online on the Voter Service Portal or offline through Booth Level Officers (BLOs).

The Complete Election Cycle

  • 1. Delimitation: Boundary fixing of constituencies.
  • 2. Voter Rolls: Updating the electoral roll (voter list).
  • 3. Notification: ECI issues election schedule & MCC begins.
  • 4. Nomination: Candidates file and scrutinize papers.
  • 5. Campaigning: Rallies and manifesto releases. Ends 48 hrs before poll.
  • 6. Polling Day: Voters verify identity and cast votes via EVM/VVPAT.
  • 7. Counting: EVMs transported to strong rooms and counted on a fixed day.
  • 8. Results: Declaration of winners and government formation.

Election Timeline

The step-by-step journey of an Indian election cycle.

1. Announcement & MCC

The ECI announces election dates. The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) comes into effect immediately to ensure a level playing field.

2. Nominations

Candidates file their nomination papers, which are scrutinized by returning officers. Candidates also have a window to withdraw their names.

3. Campaigning

Political parties and candidates campaign to woo voters. Campaigning stops 48 hours before the polling day (silence period).

4. Polling Day

Voters cast their votes using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) and Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trails (VVPATs) at designated polling stations.

5. Counting & Results

EVMs are opened in the presence of candidate representatives. Votes are counted, and the candidate with the highest votes in a constituency is declared the winner.
